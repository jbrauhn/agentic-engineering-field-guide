The central shift is from asking an AI for an answer to engineering a system that can repeatedly produce trustworthy outcomes.

<div className="quote">A model can be brilliant and still be an unreliable production system. Agentic Engineering surrounds model capability with intent, architecture, context, tools, controls, evidence, and human judgment.</div>

<div className="grid four">
  <div className="card"><h3>AI acts faster than humans can inspect</h3><p className="muted">The answer cannot be “watch every keystroke.” We need bounded work and credible evidence.</p></div>
  <div className="card"><h3>Locally correct can be globally wrong</h3><p className="muted">A change can compile and still violate architecture, mission intent, security, or lifecycle needs.</p></div>
  <div className="card"><h3>Long work outlives one context window</h3><p className="muted">Memory, state, decisions, and provenance must survive workers and sessions.</p></div>
  <div className="card"><h3>Capability ≠ accountability</h3><p className="muted">An agent may be able to act without being the right entity to approve the result.</p></div>
</div>

<div className="callout warn"><strong>Heavier process is not more mature.</strong> Planning depth, planning method, orchestration, context strategy, review depth, and model placement are intentionally selectable. Bias toward the simplest useful pattern, preserve results, and improve future recommendations from evidence.</div>
