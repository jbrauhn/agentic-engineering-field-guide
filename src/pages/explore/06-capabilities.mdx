Agents need to act in real systems, but the AE method should not depend on whichever vendor happens to provide those systems today.

<div className="grid four">
  <div className="card"><h3>What it is</h3><p className="muted">Open capability interfaces into the organization’s ecosystem.</p></div>
  <div className="card"><h3>Why it matters</h3><p className="muted">Tools and vendors change faster than engineering principles.</p></div>
  <div className="card"><h3>Failure prevented</h3><p className="muted">Vendor-shaped architecture and brittle integrations.</p></div>
  <div className="card orange-line"><h3>Core philosophy</h3><p className="muted"><strong>Open interfaces; organizational tool choice.</strong></p></div>
</div>

<div className="quote">Direct product access, APIs, Model Context Protocol (MCP), enterprise brokers, and custom adapters can all be valid. The organization chooses the integration pattern during installation; the AE system remains portable.</div>

<div className="grid three">
  <div className="card"><h3>Planning</h3><p className="muted">Identifies the capabilities the work needs.</p></div>
  <div className="card"><h3>Governance</h3><p className="muted">Scopes which actions are available to this actor and task.</p></div>
  <div className="card"><h3>Execution</h3><p className="muted">Uses tools; results become observations and evidence.</p></div>
</div>
